The Mouse record provides information
about events happening on your device's
mouse or tap events on your device's
touch screen

Record<$Mouse> definition:
 - Signals when: a button or axis state
   changes on the mouse

   field  \179        description
 \196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\197\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196\196
    evt   \179 <number> mouse event code,
          \179 see notes for details on how
          \179 to interpret evt
   which  \179 <number> varies by evt
    btn   \179 <array<boolean>> mouse
          \179 buttons: left, middle, right
          \179 when true, button is pressed
     x    \179 <number> x screen coordinate
          \179 of mouse, see notes
     y    \179 <number> y screen coordinate
          \179 of mouse, see notes
  wheeldx \179 <number> horizontal number
          \179 of clicks of mouse wheel
  wheeldy \179 <number> vertical number
          \179 of clicks of mouse wheel

Notes:
 - The evt field on record<$Mouse>
   indicates what interesting event has
   occurred and what data has recently
   changed on the mouse. Use the LISTEN
   keyword to be notified of every evt
   that occurs

   Here is a description of the possible
   evt values and their meanings:

Notes:
 - Mouse x and y fields are measured in
   VCE Display pixels as projected onto
   the physical screen and describe the
   precise point on the Display where
   the mouse is pointing

   The coordinate system has the origin
   (x: 0, y: 0) set at the center of the
   VCE Display. Positive x values are to
   the right of center and negative x
   values are to the left. Positive y
   values are above the center and
   negative y values are below

 - The double-click mouse button event
   is semi-unreliable. For it to work at
   all, the user's double click rate has
   to meet or exceed the operating
   system's double-click speed setting.
   Also, the web browser may not issue
   double-click events on some mouse
   buttons (right button) at all due to
   competing events. Use with caution

 - Mouse wheel events measure in wheel
   'clicks', which is a unit of how far
   the mouse wheel has rolled. Positive
   clicks mean the wheel has rolled up,
   and negative clicks mean the wheel
   has rolled down

 - Most mouse wheels do not have a
   horizontal rocker and can only roll
   up and down. Therefore, the wheeldx
   field will always be 0 on those mice
   and no event 9's will be generated

   However, touchpads that can work as
   mice have no such limitation and can
   generate wheeldx 9 events as easily
   as wheeldy 10 events. "Two-finger
   scrolling" gestures generate wheeldx
   and wheeldy events on touchpads
